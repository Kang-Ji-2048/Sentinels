§1 · Observations

Every storm overflow in England

Nine water companies publish near-real-time overflow status. We pull all of them, then keep the 157 that started spilling in the last 48 hours.

Joined to live rainfall

Each spill is linked to the three nearest Environment Agency rain gauges within 10 km. Blue spills had rain nearby, so they are probably allowed.

The dry ones stand out

73 spills began when every nearby gauge saw under 0.25 mm of rain in both of the two days before. One wet gauge is enough to clear a spill, so the rule is conservative.

Helliwell Syke, Yorkshire

The longest dry-day spill in this run. Three gauges nearby recorded between 0 and 0.12 mm before it began. This is the case we follow through the agents below.

§2 · Methods

A data pipeline that hands suspicious spills to a team of agents

This is not an app. It is a scheduled Databricks job and four agents, each with one job, and every step is traced in MLflow.

Two models, on purpose

The skeptic runs on a different model family, served through Databricks. One model checking its own work tends to agree with itself.

The skeptic is a gate

If it finds an innocent explanation it believes, the case stops there. Obvious false alarms cost one cheap call, not a web search and a report.

Memory in Delta

Every verdict is written back. Over time that becomes a record of repeat offenders, and of sites whose sensors keep crying wolf.

Cited, not generated

The evidence agent only states facts a Linkup source supports, and links every one, so a journalist or regulator can check it.

§5 · Limitations

A dry-day spill is a candidate, not proof

  • The data is self-reported. Overflow status comes from the water companies' own monitors.
  • Rain gauges are sparse. A shower between gauges can make a legal spill look dry. That's why the skeptic exists and every case carries a confidence score.
  • Some permits allow dry-weather discharges. Only the Environment Agency can decide a breach.
  • No report is sent automatically. A human reads and approves every one.
